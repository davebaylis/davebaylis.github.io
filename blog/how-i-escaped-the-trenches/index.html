How I escaped the querying trenches and fell in love with writing again

People occasionally ask me where the idea for Cal Chance came from. The answer I’d like to give is the spinal tap version. He’s just me turned up to eleven, and to a point that’s true. The humour is definitely me, as is the way of viewing the world; I’m not sure I could write about someone else. Or would want to.

But he started as a happy accident. I’ve just finished a novel that had taken me 20 something years and I was full of the naivety of every new writer. You know the sort. ‘My book is finished, now which agent should I sign with’!

I spent a good six months in the querying trenches and I’ll be honest; I nearly gave up. Kudos to anyone who carries on but when it started to affect my mental health I knew it was time to get out.

What I quickly realised was that it had made me fall out of love with writing. And the antidote to that, of course, is to start writing.

So, and this is my big admission, I asked ChatGPT to come up with a character name. From there I’d committed to writing a short story. Maybe a few thousand words was my plan. And then I met Cal Chance. Or maybe found myself.

The story for ‘The Kings of Clark County’ came from Agatha Christie’s ‘And then there were none’ – what if Cal had to find ten people called Elvis Presley in Las Vegas.

A year later I’ve sold almost 1,000 copies of ‘The Man with the Blue Suede Shoes’, have ‘Face Value’ due out on November 10th, with ‘The Ghost of Elvis Presley’ due in January.

All because I wanted to fall in love with writing again.

And the book that started it all off? The one that took twenty something years to complete. Well, you’ll all get the chance to meet Chester Herding next year when I (finally!) release ‘The Emperor of the Desert’.

No agent required.
